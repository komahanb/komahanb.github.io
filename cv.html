Research

Research Objective

To enable engineers to design cost-effective aerospace systems that are robust and reliable, I undertake the development of simulation infrastructure that manages the connectivity between mathematics and physics, to enact multidisciplinary physics-constrained optimization under uncertainty methods that guard the design against stochastic variability introduced by manufacture, operation and environment. The design’s optimality in terms of size, shape, topology, trajectory and control offers robustness to the aerospace industry and reliability to certification authorities across aircraft, rotorcraft and space systems.

Graduate Research Assistant

Georgia Institute of TechnologyAtlanta, Georgia, United States

Project
Development of Discrete Adjoint Capability for Rotorcraft Comprehensive Code
Funding
National Institute of Aerospace, NASA Langley Research Center
  • The implementation of time-dependent discrete adjoint capabilities in the TACS and DYMORE flexible multibody dynamics frameworks for rotorcraft optimization.
  • The Structural Solver Abstraction Layer (SSAL), an interface for flexible multibody dynamics implementations. It evaluates the residuals of the governing Euler–Lagrange equations, their machine-accurate state-variable derivatives (Jacobians) and their design-variable derivatives, and the functions of interest used in rotorcraft structural optimization. The SSAL interfaces with CFD solvers for coupled aeroelastic simulations and for the standalone dynamics of a helicopter blade hub assembly.
  • A modular implementation of implicit time-marching schemes for the differential-algebraic equations of multibody dynamics, with their discrete adjoints for time-dependent sensitivity analysis: multistep schemes (backward differences, Adams–Bashforth–Moulton), the single-step Newmark scheme and multistage diagonally implicit Runge–Kutta integrators.
  • A general-purpose algebra library with classical and Krylov iterative solvers for linear systems, eigenvalue solvers and nonlinear system solvers.
  • A probabilistic-space library that provides quadrature nodes and basis functions for normal, uniform and exponential distributions, with tensorization schemes for higher-dimensional uncertainty quantification.
  • Stochastic-TACS, a finite element and adjoint derivative framework for optimization under uncertainty. It supports non-intrusive sampling, projection and intrusive Galerkin projection, through the principle of sampling at different hierarchical levels that extends a deterministic finite element framework to a stochastic one. The demonstrations cover static and time-dependent problems in flexible multibody dynamics and a flexible manipulator modeled on the Space Shuttle’s Canadarm.
