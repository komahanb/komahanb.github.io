Flight Simulator

The Flight Simulator designs a light aircraft and flies it as one rigid body. The wing, the tailplane and the fin are NURBS surfaces, and the fuselage is a series of exact circular sections. Moving a control point updates the mass, the centre of mass and the static margin at once.

Each flight starts in trim, which an optimization finds as the pitch, elevator deflection and thrust at which nothing accelerates. The equations of motion are then integrated in time by the second-order backward differentiation formula.

Seven questions lead through flight dynamics. They cover the phugoid and its period against Lanchester’s estimate, the short-period mode and the linearity of a larger disturbance. They also cover the elevator’s control of speed and height, a centre of mass behind the neutral point, a bank that does not undo itself and a kick of the rudder.
