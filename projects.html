Projects

Simulators that a reader can open in the browser, each built on the same simulation infrastructure as the research. Each one designs a vehicle from its control points, explains its model in a guide, and replays flights recorded by the program. Running a new flight sends the setup to the machine where the programs run, which only readers with access to that machine can do.

Flight Simulators A and B

The flight simulators design a light aircraft and fly it. The wing, the tailplane and the fin are NURBS surfaces, and the fuselage is a series of exact circular sections. Moving a control point updates the mass, the centre of mass and the static margin at once.

The two pages fly the same aircraft at two levels of fidelity. Flight Simulator A flies it as one rigid body. Flight Simulator B flies the airframe and its four control surfaces, the elevator, the rudder and the two ailerons, as rigid bodies on hinges, each turned by a servo, so that it also reports the hinge moments and the work of the servos.

Each flight starts in trim, which an optimization finds as the pitch, elevator deflection and thrust at which nothing accelerates. The equations of motion are then integrated in time, by default by a fourth-order diagonally implicit Runge–Kutta method.

Its missions lead through flight dynamics. They cover the phugoid and its period against Lanchester’s estimate, the short-period mode and the linearity of a larger disturbance. They also cover the elevator’s control of speed and height, a centre of mass behind the neutral point, a bank that does not undo itself and a kick of the rudder.

Satellite Simulators A and B

The satellite simulators design a spacecraft and fly it in orbit about the Earth. Each solar panel is a NURBS surface and the bus is a series of exact circular sections, with point masses for the thruster, the payload and the tip of a gravity-gradient boom. Moving a control point shows at once whether the spacecraft, pointed at the Earth, is stable in the gravity gradient.

The orbit feels the Earth’s flattening and a thin, free-molecular atmosphere. The attitude feels the gravity gradient, the air, three reaction wheels, the torquers and the thruster. A mission may lose a stretch of a panel, fail a control or stick it on, or thicken the air with a storm.

The two pages fly the same spacecraft at two levels of fidelity. Satellite Simulator A flies it as one rigid body, each wheel’s angular momentum known from its motor. Satellite Simulator B flies each reaction wheel as a body of its own on a revolute joint to the bus. Flying one mission on both pages checks one level of fidelity against the other.
