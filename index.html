Research Objective

To enable engineers to design cost-effective aerospace systems that are robust and reliable, I undertake the development of simulation infrastructure that manages the connectivity between mathematics and physics, to enact multidisciplinary physics-constrained optimization under uncertainty methods that guard the design against stochastic variability introduced by manufacture, operation and environment. The design’s optimality in terms of size, shape, topology, trajectory and control offers robustness to the aerospace industry and reliability to certification authorities across aircraft, rotorcraft and space systems.

Where the work lands

Aircraft, rotorcraft and space systems

My passion lies in building simulation frameworks that model aerospace vehicles and their components in equilibrium with their environment and their operating conditions. My motivation is to give their designers the statistics of the quantities of interest and the sensitivities of those quantities to each design choice. I therefore undertake, and take part in, the development of scalable, flexible and robust computational frameworks, both for scientific computing and for the design of fixed-wing aircraft, rotorcraft and space systems. Four simulators, two of an aircraft and two of a spacecraft, let a reader shape a vehicle and fly it.
